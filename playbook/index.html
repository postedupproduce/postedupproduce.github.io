Five things that matter most

We've run since mid-October 2026. Every number here comes from our own order sheet, site visit log and Instagram insights.

  1. Personal asks sell. Posts help them spread. Of our 15 paid orders, 1 came from an Instagram link, and it came through a Story. Our biggest post, 13,913 views, produced 1 link tap.
  2. Reach comes from reshares, not the algorithm. About 80% of our Reel views came through Stories. The Reels tab sent under 4%.
  3. Every audience gets its own page and tracked link. A partner, a group or one buyer sees their name, and we see who came and who ordered.
  4. One fixed weekly rhythm. Orders open Monday, close 9 PM the night before, everything prepaid, numbers logged the same night.
  5. Change one thing at a time. Paid orders decide. Views, likes and shares don't.

If you sell B2B, read 1 and 3 twice. Your buyers are a short list of named people, which is exactly what this playbook is built for.

    Rollout

    Launch small and private, then go public

    1. Invite-only beta. Friends and family first, a private account, a "Beta" label on the site.
    2. Teaser before the ask. A Reel from a personal account. Comment a keyword, get the link by DM.
    3. Private drops. A password page with one limited item and a deadline, like a $30 Halloween mystery bag.
    4. A page per partner. A wine shop, a yoga studio, a newsletter writer and a restaurant each got their own.
    5. Public flip. On Nov 13 the account went public, pinned 3 posts and set one bio link.
    6. One piece first. For any set, finish one piece, get the OK, then build the rest the same way.
    7. A stop rule written in advance. By Nov 21: 2+ paid orders from Instagram, keep investing. 1, cheap layer only. 0, texts only. Result: exactly 1. We dropped to the cheap layer (Friday last-call Stories and a Saturday photo) and re-check on Dec 12.
    Honest log

    What worked, what broke, what we changed

      Fits B2B best

      A page for every account

      Every partner or buyer gets a page with their name on it, built from one reusable template with a different look each time. People act when something was clearly made for them. Try one:

      Built for you

      List by Thursday · quote Friday · prepay by 9 PM Friday · we text before any swap.

      • Their look: colors taken from their own space. Never their photos without asking.
      • Their tag: each page counts visits and carts under that account.
      • A quote sheet, not a price list: pick items and plates per week, see a running range (estimate −10% to +15%, rounded to $5) and the amount due up front.
      • One flexible term where it wins the account: for one restaurant we front up to $100 of the shopping, with the rest prepaid.
      Steal these

      Messages, rewritten for business buyers

      Short, in your own voice, approved before they go. Asks go out Sunday evening or Friday morning, never Monday morning. Two a week, max.

      Money

      Does the day actually pay?

      Goods margin holds near 32% for us. Drive time and hours decide whether a day makes money. Preset: our first full Saturday, Oct 17.

      True result for the day
      $0

      Five money lessons

      1. Markup by category, written down. Ours: produce 1.6×, mushrooms and cheese 1.45×, bread 1.35×, meat and eggs 1.33×, pantry 1.27×.
      2. Count the whole day. Better prices didn't fix our losing days. More orders per stop did.
      3. Perks don't stack. 10% off plus free delivery left two boxes at about $2 profit each. Now it's one or the other.
      4. Repeat orders beat new ones. At one point: 5 orders, 0 reorders. Five standing customers fill a day.
      5. Ask suppliers for a standing-order discount. 10% off cost on a $50 box adds $3.30 a box.

      Rules we don't break

      • Nothing customer-facing goes out without the owner's OK. Website fixes can ship on their own.
      • Never name suppliers in public. Describe them by region or kind.
      • Only claim what's verified. Every job ends with what it was checked against.
      • Founders stay private on public pages: business email and a form, no personal phone.
      • Sign as a team, say "we." Walk the site as a stranger after every change.
