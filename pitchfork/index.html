The gap

Great food, sold during working hours.

For households

Chicago's best farmers markets run weekday and Saturday mornings. People with jobs, kids or long commutes miss them, and grocery-store produce has traveled much farther.

For farms

Small farms only sell to whoever walks up during market hours. Every household that can't make it is a sale they never see. In grocery stores, farmers get about 18¢ of each food dollar (USDA Food Dollar Series, 2024).

The opening

A trusted local shopper can turn one trip to market into dozens of households served. The farmer gets their full stand price, and our fee is built into what customers pay.

How it works

Order by the night before. Picked that morning.

Order

Pick a box or build your own

Shopper's Choice boxes, or groups like "sourdough loaf" or "steak night." Prepaid by Zelle or Apple Pay; orders close 9 PM the night before.

Shop

Hand-picked at market

Our shopper chooses the best example of each group that morning, from farms within 150 miles of the Loop.

Post up

Meet-up or delivery

Pick up at a set neighborhood spot and time ("The Post-Up") in Goose Island, Hyde Park or Rogers Park, or get same-day Wednesday delivery. Order status updates by email.

Guarantee

Fair if something sells out

Anything unavailable becomes a credit toward the next order, a refund on request, or a swap if the customer says swaps are OK.

Built and running

  • Order site with boxes, build-your-own item groups and plain-language order terms
  • Orders land in a shared order sheet with a shopping list, weekly P&L and customer list
  • Status emails at each step, from "order received" to "ready at the Post-Up," plus an order tracking page
  • A 5-order cap per market day with a waitlist, so quality holds while the system is tested
  • Feedback, meeting requests and after-pickup ratings collected on the site
  • Visits tracked by link source, so we know which outreach converts
  • Customers are voting on the logo through Oct 24

What week one taught us

  • Orders run bigger than planned. About $95 on average, double the model's $47. Early buyers are mostly friends and family, so the model keeps the lower figure until the full pilot is in.
  • Personal texts convert; group posts don't. The first group announcement got no orders. One-to-one links got the first-week orders.
  • All three meet-up stops were used. Spreading out costs time, which is why the plan narrows to one zone.

Learning from those who tried

Local food delivery fails on logistics, not demand.

Several well-funded services tried to bring local food to the door. The ones that pulled back ran out of margin on delivery, not out of customers.

Pulled back

Door-to-door, every day, citywide

Farmigo's consumer service, Good Eggs' expansion cities and Peapod in the Midwest all wound down. Spread-out routes and thin margins made each order cost too much to deliver.

Survived

Fixed days and pickup points

The services still standing batch orders on set days and use free pickup spots, or grew big enough to run their own fleet.

Posted Up's design

Two market days, set meet-up windows, one zone

Orders close the night before, everything is shopped in one trip, and most handoffs happen at a single Post-Up. Delivery stays a paid add-on.

Risks, named plainly

What could go wrong, and the plan for each.

  • Farms won't discount. Start with farms that already sell to restaurants; standing weekly orders give them a reason to.
  • Demand is seasonal. Holiday gift boxes and shelf-stable pantry goods carry income past the outdoor season.
  • One person is the bottleneck. Farms pre-pack, one zone, and a paid helper above 25 orders a day.
  • Food safety and licensing. Cold chain for meat and dairy, licensed shared-kitchen space, and city licensing confirmed before scaling.
  • Prices land near farms' own CSAs. Compete on convenience, gifting and curation; wholesale pricing protects margin.
  • Early buyers are friends and family. Week-one order sizes may not hold with strangers. The model uses half the observed average until the full pilot is in.
  • The model is still a model. Every modeled figure here is replaced with pilot data in December.
